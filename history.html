Lent and César Chávez

Two Spanish immersion schools, from 2019 to PPS’s 2031 projections

Lent and César Chávez are the two schools that show what the scenarios would mean for Spanish immersion. Lent is the precedent. In 2023 PPS moved Bridger’s Spanish program into Lent and moved Lent’s neighborhood program out, making it an all-immersion school. César Chávez is the plan’s main new hub. In both scenarios it becomes a K-5 regional Spanish immersion school, taking in the programs from Beach, Sitton and James John. Its neighborhood students move to Rosa Parks and its middle grades to George.

1. Enrollment since 2019

Sources: Portland State University Population Research Center, Portland Public Schools Enrollment Forecasts, 2022-23, 2023-24 and 2026-27 editions (Appendix C / Table 5.5; archive). Method details: Sources & methods.

Before the move, the Spanish programs at Bridger and Lent together had 319 students in grades K-5 (2022-23). The combined program at Lent opened with 273 and has 256 today. César Chávez’s Spanish program has held steady at about 310 students, while its neighborhood program has shrunk from 230 to 143. (The drop from 2020-21 to 2021-22 at Lent and Bridger is their middle grades moving out, not lost students.)

2. Lent after the 2023 move

Enrollment fell short of every projection. PPS’s 2023 plan expected Lent to reach “an estimated 527 students in 2025–26” (Green, T. L., & Hanson, H. (2026, June 29). Equity audit evaluation of Portland Public Schools’ Southeast Enrollment and Program Balancing initiative: School-by-school analysis (PPS SEGC documents), p. 34). PSU’s June 2023 forecast, built with the move, had Lent at 291 that year (Portland State University Population Research Center, Portland Public Schools Enrollment Forecasts, 2022-23, 2023-24 and 2026-27 editions (Appendix C / Table 5.5; archive)). Lent enrolled 256. Kindergarten immersion fell about 31%, against 14% district-wide (details).

Lent no longer serves its neighborhood. In 2022-23, 63% of PPS students living in Lent’s area attended Lent. After the conversion that fell to 30%, mostly by design: Lent’s non-immersion neighborhood children were assigned to Marysville. Today 31% of Lent’s students live in its area. Lent’s students are now 68% Latino, while PPS students living in Lent’s area are 34% Latino (PPS, School/Neighborhood Enrollment by Ethnicity & Program, October 2025 (School rows; PPS Data & Reporting)).

Staff and families describe a hard transition. “A lot of the parents who pushed from Bridger for their kids to come to the school didn’t end up staying…they felt like it was out of their way [and that] their students didn’t feel supported” (Green, T. L., & Hanson, H. (2026, June 29). Equity audit evaluation of Portland Public Schools’ Southeast Enrollment and Program Balancing initiative: School-by-school analysis (PPS SEGC documents), p. 39). After 2022-23, 63% of Lent’s staff left (p. 39). More accounts are on the Equity page.

3. César Chávez today

César Chávez is a K-8 school with a Spanish immersion program and a smaller neighborhood program. In October 2025 it had 195 immersion students in grades K-5 and 105 in grades 6-8, plus 143 in its neighborhood program. 38% of its immersion students live in its own attendance area, and 74% of PPS students living in its area attend it, up from 63% in 2019-20.

4. What the scenarios would do

Sources: Portland State University Population Research Center, Portland Public Schools Enrollment Forecasts, 2022-23, 2023-24 and 2026-27 editions (Appendix C / Table 5.5; archive); PPS, How Could Your School Be Impacted? school guides (“PPS Family-Friendly School Guide”, per-school PDFs dated October 8, 2026), page 4, projected enrollment 2027-28 to 2031-32 (page). Method details: Sources & methods.

PPS projects Lent at 401 students in 2027-28 once Atkinson’s program arrives, against 256 with no changes. Its guide says the move “brings Lent’s student numbers above steady target levels (400+ students)” (PPS, How Could Your School Be Impacted? school guides (“PPS Family-Friendly School Guide”, per-school PDFs dated October 8, 2026), page 4, projected enrollment 2027-28 to 2031-32 (page)). That would still be 57% of Lent’s capacity of 707 (PPS, Long-Range Facility Plan 2021, Volume 1, p. 53 (elementary) and p. 57 (K-8), functional capacity (PDF); compiled in meub/pps-data by Alex Meub (MIT license)), below PPS’s 70–100% space-use goal.

César Chávez would go from 413 students with no changes to 541, all in Spanish immersion, which is 92% of its capacity of 589. The guide projects its students in poverty falling from 66% to 48% and its share of students from underserved racial groups from 82% to 69%. Those projections assume most families follow their programs. In the moves where it can be measured, PPS’s projections have about 80% of students arriving (Equity §5). After the 2023 move, Lent ended up at about half of the 527 PPS projected.

NoteCaveats
  • Enrollment is PSU’s count of October enrollment, kindergarten through grade 12, as first reported in each forecast edition. PPS’s own totals for Lent include pre-kindergarten (308 in 2023-24).
  • Lent and Bridger were K-8 schools until 2021-22, so earlier totals include middle grades.
  • Capture rates and demographics are whole-school or whole-area figures. PPS doesn’t publish race or income for immersion programs alone.
  • PPS’s projections are from school guides dated October 8, 2026 and labeled drafts. Capacities are from PPS’s 2021 facility plan.
