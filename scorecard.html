Demand & scorecard

Where families apply for immersion, and what each scenario does to it

Demand: programs move away from where families apply

Families who want a kindergarten immersion seat apply through the PPS school-choice lottery (the exception is the neighborhood families of all-immersion Lent and Rigler; see the note below). The lottery reports show how many families applied to each program, how many got an offer and how many were waitlisted. Averaged over the last five lotteries (2022-23 to 2026-27), the Spanish programs that would move include the two with the most applications and longest waitlists in the district, Atkinson and Beach, and they would move to schools that have drawn far fewer applicants.

Sources: PPS Enrollment & Transfer, Elementary School / Kindergarten Lottery Results Summary, lotteries for 2022-23 through 2026-27 (prior lottery results); PPS board packet for October 6, 2026: scenario maps and Rightsizing Update: Scenario Release memo, as digitized and transcribed in pps-explorer by Jason Brown. Schools with no orange dot (Rigler, Boise-Eliot/Humboldt, Vestal) have no immersion lottery today: Boise-Eliot/Humboldt and Vestal would be new sites, and Rigler fills its Spanish program without appearing in the lottery reports.

WarningLottery numbers understate demand at whole-school immersion schools

At Lent and Rigler, every student is in Spanish immersion, so children from the school’s own neighborhood enroll without entering the lottery; only families from outside the neighborhood apply. In 2025-26 Lent enrolled 43 immersion kindergartners but made 14 lottery offers, and Rigler enrolled 50 with no lottery at all. So Lent’s orange dot above counts only out-of-neighborhood applicants and is not directly comparable with the sending schools, which enroll about as many kindergartners as they offer lottery seats.

Sources: PPS, Enrollment by Grade and Program Type, October 2024 and October 2025 (PPS Data & Reporting); PPS Enrollment & Transfer, Elementary School / Kindergarten Lottery Results Summary, lotteries for 2022-23 through 2026-27 (prior lottery results). Method details: Sources & methods.

A precedent: Bridger’s Spanish program moved to Lent in 2023

PPS has consolidated Spanish immersion before. Until 2022-23 it ran at both Bridger and Lent, each a K-5 school with an immersion and a neighborhood program. In 2023-24 Bridger’s program moved to Lent, which became all-immersion (Bridger became the K-8 Bridger Creative Science). Before the move the two schools had about 55 Spanish immersion students per grade, roughly three kindergarten classes. Lent has since enrolled 38 immersion kindergartners a year on average (2024-25 and 2025-26), two classes: about 31% fewer. Measured the same way district-wide (the October 2022 average across grades K-5 against kindergarten in 2024-25 and 2025-26), enrollment fell about 14%; kindergarten to kindergarten, the district fell 9%.

This is an estimate: before 2024-25 PPS publishes immersion counts by school but not by grade, so “per grade” is the school’s immersion total divided by six grades. District-wide, kindergarten classes run smaller than grades 1–5, so an all-grades average likely overstates the old kindergarten size; that is why the district comparison above uses the same all-grades baseline. Lottery applications can’t be used for this comparison, because Lent’s neighborhood families stopped applying once it became all-immersion (see the note above).

Who is waitlisted

The 2026-27 kindergarten lottery breaks each program’s applicants down by where they live and their language background. At the programs with the longest waitlists, the families turned away are mostly English-speaking families from the surrounding area, the families a move puts farther away. Native Spanish speakers, who get priority, are almost always offered seats.
