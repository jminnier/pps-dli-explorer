The scenarios change far more schools than the ones that close. In Scenario A, 66 of PPS's 81 schools would close, send students away, take in students or change boundaries. In Scenario B, 59 would. This page counts those schools and the students in them. It also looks at Title I and SUN schools, and at whether the moves would sort students by race.

All numbers use October 2025 enrollment. We follow PPS's school guides to see where each school's students would go. "Underserved racial groups" is PPS's term for Latino, Black, Native American, Pacific Islander and multiracial students of other ancestries.

## In brief {#sec-brief}

- **Schools.** In Scenario A, 14 schools close and 32 schools take in students from closed schools or moved programs. In Scenario B, 11 close and 27 take in students.
- **Students.** The groups of students that would change buildings in Scenario A enroll about 6,940 students today (5,983 in B). This number shows the size of the change, not a count of the children who will move. The changes start in 2027-28 or later, so many of today's students will have moved on by then. The schools that would take them in enroll about 9,804 more students today. Those students stay put, but their school changes around them.
- **Title I schools.** All 6 closing Title I schools are the same in both scenarios: Beach, Buckman, James John, Marysville, Peninsula and Woodmere. PPS's own guides project that Brentwood Middle School would lose Title I status in both scenarios.
- **SUN schools.** 9 SUN Community Schools close in Scenario A (8 in B). In Scenario A, 6 of them are sites the county planned to cut in 2026 and then saved.
- **Separation by race.** Across the district, the plan would not make schools more separated by race. Locally, it would. Where immersion programs gather into one school, Latino and white students who now share a building would attend different buildings. Where neighborhood schools merge, as at Sabin and MLK Jr., separation falls.

## 1. How many schools and students the plan changes {#sec-count}

Most schools that change don't close. They take in students, send some students away, or both. A school "takes in students" when a closing school, an immersion program or a grade moves into it. A school "sends students away" when it keeps its building but loses a program or its middle grades.

## 2. Title I schools {#sec-title1}

Title I is federal money for schools that serve many students from low-income families. PPS explains its rule this way: "In order to be identified as a Title I school, at least 33% of K-8 students need to be receiving support services from the state." PPS lists 35 Title I schools for 2026-27. Three of them are "bridge" schools: Arleta, Atkinson and Roosevelt. PPS says bridge schools "have recently exited Title I eligibility but continue to receive a limited allocation of Title I funds for the 26-27 school year."

31 of the 35 Title I schools would close, send students away, take in students or change boundaries in Scenario A (29 in B).

- **Closing.** Beach, Buckman, James John, Marysville, Peninsula and Woodmere close in both scenarios.
- **Taking in students.** 19 Title I schools take in students in Scenario A.
- **Losing Title I status.** PPS's guides project that Brentwood Middle School would no longer be a Title I school. Students from closing Sellwood Middle School would arrive, and PPS projects Brentwood's share from underserved racial groups would fall from 46% to 27%. In Scenario A, PPS also projects that Markham would lose Title I status after taking in most of Stephenson's students.
- **Gaining Title I status.** PPS projects that Arleta, now a bridge school, would be a full Title I school again after taking in Marysville's students.

PPS has not said what happens to Title I money when a school closes or merges. We found no statement in the scenario memo, the school guides or the rightsizing FAQ (searched October 11, 2026). @tbl-plan-follow shows where students from closing Title I and SUN schools would go.

## 3. SUN Community Schools {#sec-sun}

SUN Community Schools are run by Multnomah County with partner groups. The county says they have "After school programs", "Links to food, shelter, and help with energy bills" and "Parent and family activities and events". 43 PPS schools are SUN sites on the county's August 2026 list.

- **Closing.** In Scenario A, 9 SUN schools close: Beach, Buckman, Creston, James John, Marysville, Peninsula, Rose City Park, Sabin and Woodmere. In Scenario B, 8 close.
- **Sites the county just saved.** In spring 2026 the county planned to end SUN at nine sites. It "determined it would cut sites at PPS using demographic and poverty data," a county spokeswoman told Willamette Week. In June the county board voted to restore full SUN funding. 6 of those nine schools close in Scenario A: Beach, Buckman, Creston, Peninsula, Rose City Park and Sabin. In Scenario B, 5 close.
- **Taking in students.** 20 SUN schools take in students in Scenario A.

Neither PPS nor the county has said whether a SUN site moves with its students when a school closes. Some students from SUN schools would go to schools without one. For example, Buckman's students would go to Abernethy and Sunnyside, and Beach's neighborhood students to Chief Joseph. None of those three is a SUN school (@tbl-plan-follow).

## 4. How schools that take in students would change {#sec-receiving-race}

Many schools that take in students would see a large change in who attends. The biggest changes come where a school's own program leaves and a different group of students arrives.

- **MLK Jr.** MLK Jr.'s Mandarin program moves out and all of Sabin's students move in. Sabin is 63% white, and MLK Jr.'s neighborhood program is 45% Black. Black students would go from 31% of MLK Jr. to 22%, and white students from 24% to 46%.
- **Atkinson.** Its Spanish program (70% Latino) moves to Lent, and Creston's students arrive. Latino students would go from 38% to 12% of the school. White students would go from 53% to 71%.
- **Vestal (Scenario A).** Rose City Park's Vietnamese program arrives. Asian students would go from 6% to 30% of the school. Black students would go from 13% to 8%.
- **Scott.** Scott's Spanish program moves to Rigler. In Scenario A, Rose City Park's neighborhood program (67% white) arrives. Latino students would go from 33% to 18%, and white students from 38% to 48%. In Scenario B, Scott keeps only its neighborhood program, and Black students would go from 10% to 18%.
- **César Chávez.** It becomes a Spanish immersion school, and its neighborhood program moves out. Black students would go from 12% of the school to 1%. See [Equity §3](equity.qmd#sec-black).
- **Rosa Parks.** It takes in Peninsula and César Chávez's neighborhood program. Its share from underserved racial groups would fall from 82% to 65%.

## 5. Would the moves sort students by race? {#sec-segregation}

Across the district, no. In the places where immersion programs gather into one school, yes. Two measures help show this.

The **dissimilarity index** measures how unevenly two groups are spread across schools. It is the share of one group that would have to change schools for every school to have the same mix. 0 means every school has the same mix; 1 means the two groups never attend the same school.

The answer depends on what you count as one school. Some buildings already hold two very different programs. At Scott, the Spanish immersion program is 47% Latino and the neighborhood program is 20% Latino. Today those children share one building, its principal and its after-school programs. So we measure two ways:

- **Each building as one unit.** Children in the same building count as attending the same school.
- **Each program as one unit.** An immersion program and a neighborhood program count as separate, even in one building.

When a program moves to its own building, the building measure goes up but the program measure barely changes. The split was already there. What changes is whether the children share a building and its services.

## 6. What this estimate can't show {#sec-limits}

- **It uses today's students to measure the change.** We move October 2025 students all at once, as the school guides describe. The real changes start in 2027-28 or later, and many of today's students will have moved on. For example, Sellwood Middle School closes in 2028, after all of its current students have left 8th grade. So our counts show the size of each change, not the children who will move.
- **School sizes.** Our sizes can differ from PPS's projections. Brentwood grows from 447 to 808 students here, but PPS projects 457 for 2031-32, when all the changes are in place. PPS's projections include future boundary effects and enrollment trends. We use PPS's projections as a check on the race and ethnicity shares, which are what this page compares.
- **Grades.** PPS reports race by school and program, not by grade. We assume each grade has the same mix as its school or program.
- **Boundary shares.** Where PPS gives a share of a boundary (half of Buckman, 70% of Maplewood), we apply it to enrolled students. Sellwood's split between Hosford and Brentwood follows its feeder schools' enrollment.
- **Future students.** Feeder changes that move only future middle schoolers, such as Kelly to Harrison Park, are not counted as moves.
- **Small programs.** Some special education classrooms and the Deaf and Hard of Hearing program move. We don't show them separately, because PPS's race report doesn't list them.

Details are in [Sources & methods](methods.qmd#school-changes).
