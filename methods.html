Sources & methods

Where every number comes from, how it was processed, and what it can’t tell you

Credits

This site builds on pps-explorer by Jason Brown (@browniefed), an interactive map of the PPS boundary scenarios. Its georeferenced attendance areas and school locations (digitized from the PPS scenario map PDFs) and its transcription of the October 2026 board memo (closures, program moves and grade changes) are the basis for every scenario comparison on this site. pps-explorer in turn credits ppsdata.info by Alex Meub (meub/pps-data).

This is an independent analysis, not a PPS publication. Where anything here differs from PPS documents, the PPS documents are authoritative.

How each source was processed

1. Immersion enrollment by school (Language Immersion Track Profiles)

Each PDF lists every school with an immersion program on two lines: counts, then the same counts as a percentage of the school’s total October enrollment. The columns are: total enrollment; students who are multilingual learners (ML) or in immersion; neither; total immersion students; immersion students by language (Spanish, Mandarin, Japanese, Russian, Vietnamese); total ML students; ML students by primary language (Spanish, Chinese, Japanese, Russian, Vietnamese, Somali, Other); students who are both ML and in immersion; and a final percentage.

  • Reading the numbers. Text was extracted with pypdf. In the 2021-22 and 2022-23 files the PDF’s text layer runs adjacent numbers together (four zeros print as “0000”). Each school’s digits were split every possible way, and the split kept is the one where every count matches its printed percentage of enrollment (within rounding, ±0.51 points) and the row identities hold: immersion total = sum of the five immersion languages; ML total = sum of the ML languages; “ML or immersion” = immersion + ML − both; “neither” = enrollment − “ML or immersion”. Exactly one split must pass, or the script stops.
  • ML-language columns. In 2021-22 the text layer sometimes lists the ML-language counts out of column order; they are matched to columns by their printed percentages. In 21 school-years (2021-22 and 2022-23) the ML-language counts in the text layer don’t add up to the school’s own ML total; for those rows the ML-language breakdown is left blank and only the checked totals are kept. No figure on this site uses the ML-language breakdown.
  • Mislabelled column. The last column is headed “Percent of ML in Immersion (Both ML and Imm / ML)”, but the printed values are both ÷ total immersion (Ainsworth 2025: 38 ÷ 287 = 13%, whereas 38 ÷ 59 = 64%). It is stored as pct_immersion_ml.
  • Date typo. The 2023-24 file’s title reads “October 2022”; it is treated as 2023-24, matching the PPS archive’s label and its contents.
  • Page breaks. Where a school’s percentage line falls on the next page (Richmond, 2025-26), it is joined back to its count line.

2. Enrollment by grade and program type

Each school is listed by program (e.g. “Spanish Immersion”, “Neighborhood Program”) with enrollment in each grade PreK–12 and a total. Multi-program schools end with a school total row, and schools are grouped into sections (elementary, middle, high, alternative, community-based, special services, charter) with section totals and a district total.

  • Cells are read by position: the text layer prints every grade cell, blank where a grade isn’t served. Counts over 999 contain commas, and one wrapped program name is rejoined.
  • Checks (all pass for both years): each row’s grades sum to its total; each school’s program rows sum to its school total in every grade; schools sum to their section totals; sections sum to the district total (44,086 in October 2024; 43,479 in October 2025).
  • Whole-school immersion. Lent, Rigler and Richmond print as a single unlabelled row. Source 1 shows all of their students in immersion, so they are tagged Spanish, Spanish and Japanese immersion respectively.
  • Cross-check: immersion enrollment summed from this report equals the immersion totals in source 1 for every school in both years.

3. Enrollment by language and school

For each school and program, students by home-language group (English, Spanish, Vietnamese, Chinese, Russian, Somali/Maay-Maay, Japanese, Other), as counts and percentages. “Chinese” includes Cantonese, Mandarin and other Chinese languages (report footnote).

  • Empty cells are omitted from the PDF text, so each number is assigned to a column by its x-position on the page, using column boundaries taken from the header row.
  • Checks (all pass): language counts sum to the program’s enrollment; every count matches its printed percentage (±0.51 points); program rows sum to the school total row; all schools sum to the district total for every language.
  • Cross-check: immersion enrollment by school and language equals source 1 exactly (64 school-years compared, 0 differences).

4. Lottery results

PPS publishes a results summary for each lottery: for each school and program, the number of applications, offers, waitlisted applicants and cancelled applications, and in some years the number of seats (“slots”) and applicants not placed. “Cancelled” means the applicant was placed at a program they ranked higher. Waitlists are as of the spring lottery; waitlisted applicants may still be offered a seat before the end of September.

The layout differs every year, so each file was handled separately:

  • Language of each program comes from a school-to-language list checked against the immersion sites on the status-quo map (source 8) and source 1.
  • Checks: applicant-group rows sum to the printed school total in every year that prints totals (103 school totals in 2022-23, 2023-24 and 2026-27), and the printed grand totals match (7).   • Coverage. Every elementary immersion program appears in every year except Rigler, which appears in none. Middle and high school immersion appears in only one report (a small 2024-25 table without offers), so there is no lottery measure of demand for middle-grade programs.

5. PSU enrollment forecasts

Tables 5.2–5.4 (district by grade; medium, low and high scenarios) and 5.5 (by school and program, medium scenario only) were parsed. Printed subtotals and totals were checked, allowing up to 3 students of rounding in forecast years, and the base-year actuals match source 2 (435 comparisons). Richmond is printed with a total row only; a Japanese row equal to its total was added, as the report already does for Lent and Rigler. The forecasts will feed the projections page and are not yet used elsewhere.

6. Kindergarten class size

The Class Size Detail logs give each school’s average class size overall and by grade group (kindergarten, grades 1–3, 4–5, …), as of mid-October. The kindergarten value for Lent and Bridger was copied by hand from each year’s PDF into data/class_size_kg.csv, along with the file name it came from. It averages all kindergarten classes at the school, immersion and neighborhood alike.

7. District enrollment by grade, October 2022

Read from the district summary page of the October 2022 enrollment profile: kindergarten 3,152; grades 1–5: 3,376, 3,319, 3,502, 3,485, 3,358. October 2024 and 2025 district kindergarten totals are summed from source 2.

8. Scenario sites and program moves

export_dli_tables.mjs runs the map project’s own code on its data, so these tables match the map:

  • Sites. For each scenario (status quo, A, B) and grade band (K-5, 6-8, 9-12), every school whose label on that map names an immersion program (“Rigler K-5 Spanish Immersion”; “Roseway Heights Middle School Spanish Immersion, Vietnamese Immersion”) is a site for each language named. Each scenario site is marked kept or added relative to the status quo, and status-quo sites missing from a scenario are recorded as removed. own_area records whether the school has its own attendance area on that map.
  • Moves. Every language program move in the memo transcription, with the grade band whose maps show both ends, plus one grade change that carries an immersion strand: César Chávez becomes K-5 and its grades 6-8, with their Spanish immersion students, move to George.
  • Checks. Every site removed in a scenario is explained by a memo move or grade change, and school names match source 1 (Brentwood appears as “Lane” in PPS enrollment reports).

9. Home language by census tract

  • Tables. ACS table B16001 lists Russian and Japanese separately but is published only for states; its tract and county values are blank. Tract counts therefore come from C16001, which reports Spanish, Chinese (including Mandarin and Cantonese) and Vietnamese separately. Russian is approximated by “Russian, Polish or other Slavic languages”, which in Portland also includes Ukrainian speakers. Japanese falls inside “Other Asian and Pacific Island languages” and has no tract count. School-age (5–17) counts come from B16007, which separates only Spanish, other Indo-European, Asian and Pacific Island, and other languages.
  • Which tracts. Tracts in Multnomah, Washington and Clackamas counties whose interior point (sf::st_point_on_surface) falls inside the district, defined as the union of the status-quo high school attendance areas: 135 tracts.
  • Margins of error. ACS counts are survey estimates; each estimate’s margin of error is saved alongside it in acs_language_tract.csv. Margins are large for small groups and single tracts, so tract figures are approximate; district totals are more reliable.

How each figure and table is calculated

Joining across sources

School names differ across sources (“Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.” in the memo, “MLK Jr” in enrollment reports, “Cesar Chavez” in lottery reports). All joins use a normalized key, the same one the map uses: grade and school-type words are removed, accents stripped, letters lowercased and other characters dropped, with a short alias list (MLK variants → mlkjr, Lane → Brentwood).

Kindergarten lottery demand (demand chart, waitlist chart, scorecard lottery columns, overview tile 2)

  1. For each school and lottery year, take the elementary immersion rows for kindergarten (or with no grade printed). Use the printed school total row where the year prints one; otherwise sum the applicant-group rows.
  2. Five-year average = mean over the 2022-23 to 2026-27 lotteries in which the program appears. The 2021-22 lottery is left out because its immersion rows have no grade.
  3. Overview tile 2 = applications to the Spanish programs that move in Scenario A ÷ all Spanish kindergarten applications, both summed over the same five lotteries.
  4. Counts are applications, not children: a family can apply to several programs, so one child can be waitlisted at more than one school.
  5. At Lent and Rigler (all-immersion schools with their own neighborhoods), neighborhood children enroll without applying, so lottery numbers there count only out-of-neighborhood families. Lent enrolled 43 immersion kindergartners in October 2025 but made 14 lottery offers; Rigler enrolls 50–57 a year and never appears in the lottery. At every other immersion school, including Richmond (which has no neighborhood of its own), October kindergarten enrollment is close to the number of lottery offers.

Who is waitlisted (2026-27 table)

Applicant-group rows from the 2026-27 kindergarten report, summed by residence and by the language background PPS records (English, Heritage, Native). Residence groups: NH = lives in the host school’s attendance area; RG = lives in the regional Spanish immersion area (the set of schools that feed a high-school Spanish immersion program); TR = lives elsewhere in PPS. The four Spanish programs with the most waitlisted applications are shown.

Scorecard of program moves

  • Students: October 2025 enrollment (source 2) in the moving language program at the sending school, in the grades of the move’s band (K–5 or 6–8).
  • K lottery, per year: the five-year averages above, shown only for K-5 moves.
  • Home language matches: among a program’s immersion students in October 2025 (source 3), the share whose home language is the program language; for Mandarin, the share whose home language is any Chinese language. Shown for the sending program and for the receiving school’s existing program in that language.
  • Scenario: “A & B” when the move appears in both scenarios’ memo transcription, otherwise the one it appears in.

Sites by language and grade band

Count of sites (source 8) for each language, grade band and scenario, excluding removed rows.

Distance to the nearest site (table, overview tile 4)

ACS language counts are only available for whole census tracts (about 4,000 residents each), and a tract can be over a mile across, so where people are placed inside a tract matters for a 1-mile measure.

  1. Spread each tract’s speakers over its census blocks. Each tract’s count is divided among its 2020 census blocks in proportion to block population: all residents for the age 5+ counts (2020 Census table P1), and residents under 18 for school-age Spanish speakers (P1 total minus P3 adults). This assumes speakers are spread like the population within each tract. There are 10,866 blocks in the 135 district tracts.
  2. Distance. For each block, the straight-line distance in miles from its interior point to the nearest K-5 site of that language, in Oregon North state-plane coordinates (EPSG:2838), for the status quo and each scenario.
  3. Within 1 mile = speakers in blocks within 1 mile ÷ all speakers of that language in the district. Average distance = mean distance weighted by speakers.

  • Placement. Putting each tract’s whole population at one interior point (the first version of this site) gave 59% → 35% at 1 mile. That figure was sensitive to the threshold: at half a mile it gives 12%, because several large tracts sit just inside a mile from a site. Spreading speakers over blocks gives 52% → 36% and changes more smoothly with distance; it is the figure used.
  • Survey error. Each tract’s school-age Spanish count was redrawn 2,000 times from a normal distribution with its ACS standard error (90% margin of error ÷ 1.645, floored at zero) and the share recomputed. 90% intervals: status quo 44–57%, Scenarios A and B 29–41%, drop 11–20 percentage points.
  • Plausibility of the counts. The ACS estimates 4,027 (±676) school-age Spanish speakers in the district’s tracts, 7.0% of all residents aged 5–17. PPS counted 3,081 students with Spanish as their home language in October 2025, 7.1% of its enrollment; the ACS figure is larger because it also includes children in private and charter schools.
  • Sites. Today’s K-5 Spanish sites: Ainsworth, Atkinson, Beach, César Chávez, James John, Lent, Rigler, Scott, Sitton. Scenarios A and B: Ainsworth, César Chávez, Lent, Rigler. Locations are the school icons on the PPS scenario maps (source 8).
  • Limits. Straight-line distance understates real travel, especially across freeways and rivers. The within-tract spread assumes speakers live where the general (or child) population does. Tract-level distances (access_tract.csv) are still published for mapping.

Bridger → Lent precedent

  • Immersion students per grade = each school’s Spanish immersion total (source 1) ÷ 6, since both schools served K–5 in the years used. Before October 2024 PPS doesn’t publish immersion enrollment by grade, so this is an average across grades; an actual kindergarten cohort can be larger or smaller.
  • Kindergarten (actual) = October kindergarten enrollment in Spanish immersion (source 2), 2024-25 and 2025-26.
  • Estimated kindergarten immersion classes = kindergarten immersion students (actual where published, otherwise the per-grade average) ÷ the school’s average kindergarten class size (source 6).
  • Change = average actual kindergarten immersion enrollment at Lent in 2024-25 and 2025-26 ÷ average combined per-grade immersion enrollment of Bridger and Lent in 2021-22 and 2022-23, minus 1.
  • District comparison, same baseline: the before side for Lent is an average across grades K–5, and PPS kindergarten classes are smaller than grades 1–5 (October 2022: 3,152 kindergartners vs 3,319–3,502 in each of grades 1–5). So the district comparison also uses an all-grades baseline: average district kindergarten enrollment in October 2024 and October 2025 (source 2) ÷ the average enrollment per grade across grades K–5 in October 2022 (source 7), minus 1. The kindergarten-to-kindergarten district change is reported alongside for reference.
  • Lottery applications are deliberately not used here: before 2023 Lent’s neighborhood families applied for immersion through the lottery, and afterwards they enroll directly, so before and after counts measure different groups.

Equity page

data-raw/equity_analysis.R produces equity_changes.csv (one row per closure or immersion move), equity_groups.csv (group totals), equity_home_distance.csv / equity_home_distance_summary.csv and equity_home_sensitivity.csv.

PPS’s subgroup table (source 11) is reproduced as printed. The slide gives no year and does not define a “move”; the district-wide comparison sheet labels the same totals “students who would change schools” and describes figures as 2031-32 projections unless noted. Whether students whose immersion program relocates are counted is not stated.

Groups compared.

  • Immersion program moves: for every language program move in a scenario (source 8), plus the César Chávez grades 6–8 Spanish strand moving to George: the October 2025 enrollment in that language program at the sending school, in the grades that move (source 2).
  • School closures (non-immersion students): for every school the scenario closes (source 8), the October 2025 enrollment in its non-immersion programs (source 3). Immersion students at closing schools (Beach, James John, Rose City Park in A) are counted once, under their program’s move.
  • All PPS students: October 2025 district enrollment (source 3).

Boundary changes, which move most students in PPS’s totals, are not included in either group; no public data gives the students or home languages in each area that changes.

Home language. For each immersion move, the home-language mix of the sending program’s immersion students (all grades of that program) is applied to the students who move. For closures, the mix of the school’s non-immersion students. “Non-English” is any home language other than English.

How far the school moves. Straight-line distance between school locations on the scenario maps. For a closure whose attendance area is split among several receiving schools, distances to each receiving school are weighted by the share of the closing school’s attendance area (by land area) that each receives on the scenario map; receiving areas under 2% of the closing area are ignored.

Home-to-school distance (estimate). Children are placed by census block using 2020 population under 18 (source 10).

  • Closures: blocks inside the closing school’s status-quo attendance area (same grade band). Before = distance to the closing school; after = distance to the school whose attendance area contains the block on the scenario map.
  • Immersion moves: immersion families’ addresses are not published. Each block is assigned to its nearest current immersion site of the same language and grade band (a catchment by proximity), and the blocks assigned to the sending program are used. Before = distance to the sending school; after = distance to the program’s new school. For Spanish, a second version weights blocks by school-age Spanish speakers (ACS tract counts spread over blocks by population under 18).
  • Within each change, distances are averaged over children; averages across changes are weighted by each change’s enrolled students.
  • Sensitivity (equity_home_sensitivity.csv): the immersion estimate is recomputed excluding Vietnamese (whose catchment is the whole district), for Spanish programs only, and for Spanish programs weighting blocks by school-age Spanish speakers; closures are also recomputed with Spanish-speaker weighting. The immersion increase stays between about +1.0 and +1.2 miles and the closure increase between about +0.3 and +0.5 miles. None of these versions tests the assumption about where immersion families live; the check below does.
  • Limitations: straight-line distances; proximity catchments are only a model of where immersion families live (immersion students often come from a wider area, and for languages with a single site, such as Vietnamese, the catchment is the whole district); moves of whole attendance areas between neighborhood schools by boundary change are not included.

Race and ethnicity. PPS’s School/Neighborhood Enrollment by Ethnicity & Program report gives each school’s whole-school racial composition and its percentage in immersion, not the racial composition of immersion programs, so the race/ethnicity of students in moving programs cannot be computed from public reports.

Equity page: school composition, demand and families not following

School composition (source 12). For each scenario, schools are grouped as losing an immersion program (the sending school of any program move or the César Chávez grade change), receiving an immersion program (receiving schools not also sending), and closing with no immersion program (closing schools not also sending). Each school is in one group: Beach, James John and Rose City Park, which close and lose an immersion program, are counted as losing an immersion program. Percentages are October 2025 School-row values, weighted by enrollment; the district row is PPS’s Grand Total. These describe whole schools, not their immersion programs, which PPS does not report by race or income. Clark is not printed in either year’s report and is left out (the report’s level subtotals include it).

  • Historically underserved, per the report’s footnote: “Latino, African American, Native American, Pacific Islander and Multi-Racial Other Ancestries.”
  • Direct certification: per the footnote, students the state has identified as eligible for free meals; paper applications are excluded.
  • Parsing checks: race shares sum to 98–102% per row; school enrollment matches source 2 for all 79 schools; the immersion share × enrollment matches source 1 within rounding; level subtotals plus program rows equal the grand total (43,479 in October 2025). Blank cells are read as 0 (rows with blanks still sum to about 100%).

Multilingual learners in moving programs (source 1). Students who are both multilingual learners and immersion students ÷ immersion students, summed over the sending programs (whole program, all grades), October 2025.

Demand at moving programs (source 4). Five-year average kindergarten lottery applications, offers and waitlist for each moving K-5 program (see Kindergarten lottery demand above); applications per offer is the ratio of the averages.

Families not following a moved program (estimate). Students = October 2025 enrollment in each moving program in the grades that move; kindergartners = October 2025 kindergarten immersion enrollment at each sending K-5 program. Three assumptions are applied:

  • 0%: everyone follows.
  • 7%: a figure a public commenter attributed to “PPS’s own estimate” of affected families leaving the district (October 6, 2026 board meeting, source 14). We did not find it in PPS documents or staff remarks, and it concerns leaving the district, not leaving a program. It is shown for comparison only.
  • The Bridger → Lent precedent: 1 − (Lent kindergarten immersion ÷ prior Bridger + Lent immersion per grade) ÷ (district kindergarten ÷ prior district per-grade enrollment). That is 1 − 0.69 / 0.86 ≈ 20%.

The precedent rate comes from one consolidation; other programs could fare better or worse. PPS’s equity audit (source 13) found that students already enrolled in moved programs who stayed in PPS generally followed their program, so the loss in the precedent is likely concentrated among new kindergarten families. The six-year total multiplies the yearly kindergarten figure by six and ignores later-grade entry and exit.

Forecast check (sources 5 and 15). For Lent, the 2023-24 forecast edition (June 2023, built on October 2022 enrollment) includes the move: “Bridger Spanish DLI (grades K-5) moved to Lent ES” (Appendix C, note 3). Its Lent forecasts for 2023-24 to 2025-26 are compared with PSU’s own October actuals for those years, taken from the 2026-27 edition. The 2022-23 edition predates the move (“These changes are not incorporated in these enrollment forecasts”), so its Bridger and Lent Spanish rows are added together as the pre-move program. District totals from the same editions show the size of a typical forecast miss.

  • Actuals are kindergarten through grade 12. PPS’s own school totals for Lent include pre-kindergarten (308 in October 2023 vs PSU’s 273), so the two are not mixed.
  • Text-layer checks: in the 2023-24 edition, runs of zeros merge in the text layer, and a few rows are garbled (not Lent). Those rows were decoded from school totals minus the other program rows, and the subtotals and the TOTAL row then reconcile.
  • Limits: forecasts made one or two years ahead also miss for ordinary reasons. Other schools’ Spanish programs missed by up to 10–27% in either direction (Rigler and Atkinson were under-forecast), so Lent’s miss is notable for its size and persistence, not unique.
  • No edition states an assumed share of students following a moved program. The 98% figure (314 of 319) is implied by the numbers.

Share of a moved program PPS projects to follow it (sources 5 and 16)

parse_school_guides.py reads page 4 of each guide (projected enrollment by option, 2027-28 to 2031-32; type of change; size and space goals; share within 1 mile; whole-school poverty, special education, multilingual and underserved-race shares) and the page 3 text. Checks: every school has a complete “No changes” row; option labels are from a known set; each printed row has five years; percentages are 0–100; the enrollment and school tables list the same school × option pairs. Closing schools are kept with status “closes”. Re-downloading all 82 guides later on October 8 gave identical files.

  • The guides’ “No changes” projections equal PSU’s 2026-27 forecast school totals; equity_analysis.R stops if that fails for the schools used.
  • Arrive = receiving school’s options projection − its “No changes” projection. Share arriving = arrive ÷ PSU’s forecast of the moving program in the same year.
  • Stay = sending school’s options projection − PSU’s forecast of its remaining programs, only where the sending school has no other change (Scott in Scenario B; Clark).
  • Used only where the receiving school’s sole change is the arriving program: Atkinson → Lent, Scott → Rigler, Clark → Woodstock. Arrive + stay comes to 100–111% of the program, so the split is approximate.
  • The guides state no follow rate. The shares are implied, and PPS’s model may place non-following students in ways the guides don’t show (for example, at other immersion sites).

Neighborhood capture rates (source 18)

Each report gives, for every attendance area, PPS K-12 students living there by the type of school they attend: their own neighborhood school (the capture rate), another neighborhood school, a PPS alternative, a community-based alternative, special services or a PPS charter. Pre-kindergarten is excluded. Immersion students attending their own area’s school count as captured; immersion students attending another school’s program count under “other neighborhood school” or “PPS alternative”.

  • Parsing checks: categories sum to each row’s total; printed percentages match counts (±0.51); level totals and the grand total (29,036 of 42,622 in October 2025) match the sums of their rows. Every year 2019-20 to 2025-26 uses the same layout.
  • Groups (equity_capture.csv): Southeast = the elementary areas of Abernethy, Atkinson, Bridger (Bridger Creative Science from 2023-24), Creston, Duniway, Grout, Lewis, Llewellyn, Sunnyside Environmental, Woodstock, Marysville, Kelly, Arleta, Whitman and Woodmere; Lent is shown separately; Rest of PPS = all other elementary areas. Group rates are total own-school students ÷ total residents.
  • Limits: boundaries changed in 2021-22 and 2023-24, so areas’ resident counts jump; students allowed to stay at their former school after a boundary change count as “other neighborhood school”; Creative Science (a focus school) moved into the Bridger building in 2023-24; Lent’s non-immersion neighborhood children were assigned to Marysville after 2023, which the report counts as “other neighborhood school”.

Checking the home-to-school model against where students live (source 19)

The School Enrollment by Neighborhood of Residence report gives, for each school and program, students living in the school’s own attendance area, then everyone else by the high-school area they live in (Cleveland, Franklin, Grant, Ida B. Wells-Barnett, Jefferson/Grant, Jefferson/McDaniel, Jefferson/Roosevelt, Lincoln, McDaniel, Roosevelt), plus out-of-district or undetermined. Pre-kindergarten is excluded. Parsing checks: cells sum to row totals; printed percentages match (±0.51); program rows sum to school totals and schools to section and grand totals; own-area counts equal the capture-rate report for all 53 elementary schools in each year; October 2025 school × program totals equal source 2 for all 142 rows.

check_home_distance.R places each moving program’s students on 2020 census blocks: own-area students in the school’s status-quo attendance area; others in their high-school area, outside the school’s own area. The Jefferson sub-areas have no published boundaries, so Jefferson-area blocks are assigned to whichever of the Grant, McDaniel and Roosevelt areas is nearest. Within an area, students are spread like children under 18 (area average), or over only the half of the area’s children living closest to the program’s current school (nearer half). Out-of-district and undetermined students are left out. Moves are averaged with the same weights as the model (students in the grades that move).

  • Limits: high-school areas are large, so students living outside their school’s area are placed only roughly; César Chávez’s row covers grades K-8 though only grades 6-8 move to George; straight-line distances.

Southeast seats, demand and site context (sources 2, 4, 9, 10, 19)

  • Kindergarten seats = October 2025 kindergarten enrollment (source 2): Richmond and Le Monde whole school; Lent and Atkinson Spanish immersion. Program students = all grades of the same programs. Lottery columns are the five-year averages defined above; Le Monde is a charter with its own admissions and isn’t in the PPS lottery reports.
  • Southeast Spanish means the two Spanish immersion sites in the Franklin and Cleveland high-school areas, Lent and Atkinson.
  • Miles to district edge: straight line from the school’s location on the status-quo map (source 8) to the boundary of the union of the high-school attendance areas.
  • Own area: Spanish immersion students living in the school’s own attendance area (source 19); Rigler and Lent are whole-school.
  • School-age Spanish speakers within 1.5 miles: ACS tract counts spread over census blocks by population under 18 (sources 9 and 10), summed over blocks within 1.5 miles.
  • Capacity (source 21): functional capacity = permanent and modular classrooms × PPS station size, minus set-asides, as printed in the 2021 plan. Parsing checks: every row has three counts and five utilization percentages; all 71 sites match meub/pps-data’s independent parse. Share of capacity = October 2025 K-5 enrollment (source 2), or PPS’s 2027-28 Scenario A projection (source 16), ÷ functional capacity. PPS’s operating utilization model gives different capacities for some buildings (Creston 282 without its annex; Lent 659), as noted in meub/pps-data. A “class per grade” is 6 grades × 24 students = 144. Distance from Atkinson: straight line between school locations on the status-quo map (Creston 0.8 mi, Lent 2.4 mi). Creston’s seismic status (unreinforced masonry, targeted for retrofit) is from the PPS facility data compiled in meub/pps-data and was not checked against PPS’s bond documents here.
  • Richmond’s point on the original pps-explorer map was a copy of Atkinson’s (SE 58th & Division). It has been corrected in this copy to the Census Bureau geocode of its address, 2276 SE 41st Ave, just north of Division. No figure on this site depends on Richmond’s location.
